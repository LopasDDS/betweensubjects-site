The goldfish three-second memory, and the studies that were run instead.

The claim. Goldfish are said to have a memory of three seconds, sometimes
seven. The figure is repeated everywhere and attached to no study. There is no
original experiment to cite, because there was never one: the number is
folklore, and it has long been convenient folklore, since an animal that
forgets its surroundings every few seconds cannot mind living in a bowl.

Note what that means for the shape of this. There is no finding to overturn.
The honest position is that a widely repeated claim has no evidence behind it,
and that the experiments actually run point the other way.

The goldfish study. Philip Gee, David Stephenson and Donald E. Wright,
"Temporal discrimination learning of operant feeding in goldfish (Carassius
auratus)", Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior, volume 62, issue
1, 1994, pages 1-13. Eight goldfish were trained to work a lever that released
food when pressed. The window in which the lever did anything was then
narrowed, step by step, until it worked for just one hour in every twenty-four.

What they found. The fish concentrated their lever pressing around the hour
when food was actually available, rather than pressing at random through the
day. They were anticipating the feeding time. Whatever a goldfish is doing
there, it is carrying information across many hours and acting on it.

The longer-memory study, in a different fish. Culum Brown, "Familiarity with
the test environment improves escape responses in the crimson spotted
rainbowfish, Melanotaenia duboulayi", Animal Cognition, volume 4, issue 2,
2001, pages 109-113. Rainbowfish faced with a model trawl net learned over
five runs to find the single hole in it and escape faster. Eleven months later
they were shown the trawl again, and their escape times were still short.

This is the result usually meant when people say fish remember for months. It
is worth being exact about it: those were rainbowfish, not goldfish. It does
not transfer automatically between species.

Limits that must be stated. The goldfish work is eight animals in a laboratory
operant task, and what it demonstrates is anticipation across a daily cycle -
hours, firmly, not months. The rainbowfish result is a different species,
small, and measures retention as a faster escape rather than anything one
could call recollection. Neither study was designed to measure the outer limit
of a fish's memory, and "no source exists for the three-second claim" is an
absence of evidence, which is a weaker thing than a measurement.

What this must not be heard as. It is not a claim that goldfish think like
mammals, or that any particular tank size is or is not adequate. The welfare
argument about bowls is a real argument, but it rests on oxygen, water volume,
filtration and swimming space, and it neither needs this evidence nor is
settled by it. The narrow point is only that the three-second figure is
invented, and that when anyone bothered to test it, the fish remembered.

Accurate summary: no study has ever found a three-second memory in goldfish;
the number has no source. Eight goldfish given a lever that released food for
only one hour a day learned to press it around that hour, and in a related
experiment on a different species, rainbowfish that learned to escape a model
trawl still escaped quickly when shown it eleven months later.
